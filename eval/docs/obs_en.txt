The Halvern Observatory sits on the summit of Mount Corrin at an altitude of 2,840 metres. It was founded in 1931 by the astronomer Edda Marsk, who chose the site because the sky there is clear on about 290 nights each year. Its main instrument is a 1.8-metre reflecting telescope, installed in 1962 and upgraded with a new mirror coating in 2009. The observatory employs 24 researchers and is best known for discovering the comet Marsk-Halvern in 1974. Visitors may tour the dome on the first Saturday of every month, and the tour costs 12 euros. A small library next to the dome holds the original star charts drawn by the founder.
